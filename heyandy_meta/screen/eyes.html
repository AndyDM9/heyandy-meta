2 Where might it be?

First, show Andy your thing. Then sweep the room with your camera, or a photo of the table, the shelf, the room.
Meta, live · in this browserDINOv2 ready when you are

How it works. DINOv2 (Meta's open image model; the small one with registers, Apache-2.0) turns each 14-pixel square of a photo into 384 numbers. The squares that look most like your thing say where it might be; the few best places are cut out and looked at again whole, and the closest to your thing is the answer, if it's close enough and its colour matches too (DINOv2 learned to overlook colour, so a red mug shaped like your blue one would otherwise pass). Too close to call, and Andy asks. The same idea as Andy's "this is my mug" on the robot, where DINOv2 runs on the computer beside it.

Sweeping live. Each moment of the camera gets the same check with one place at a time: DINOv2 looks at the whole view, then again at the place most like your thing, a frame or two a second on a laptop's processor, faster on its graphics chip. The tone rises and quickens as that place looks more like it. When two frames running say it's yours, Andy holds that view, looks again closely, and shows you, with the sound the robot makes when it finds something.

Private. The page downloads DINOv2 once (24 MB, from Hugging Face) and the library that runs it (from a public CDN); your photos are read here and sent nowhere. Close the page and they're gone.
