# The hackathon's rules, as published

**Read signed in on 2026-10-27** from the Cerebral Valley participant guide
(https://cerebralvalley.ai/e/meta-ai-developer-hackathon/details). The quotations are verbatim. Check the live page again
before submitting.

## Problem statements

> Your project must build towards one of the following problem statements: **Build for Your World** … **Push the
> Frontier** - Use Meta's models to tackle challenges in software, robotics, manufacturing, energy, materials and beyond.
> Give a machine a new capability, accelerate a scientific experiment, or unlock a better way to understand and control a
> physical system.

> Your project must incorporate Meta's models. Use the Muse Spark series, Muse Wonder, Muse Image Muse Voice Transcribe
> and SAM 3.1 through Meta Model API, run Meta open weights models Muse Glimmer and Muse Spark Open, and code faster with
> Muse Code coding agent.

**Ours:** Push the Frontier.

## The rules (section 4)

> **Open source:** Repositories must be public.
>
> **Team size:** A maximum of four participants per team. Solo participants are allowed.
>
> **Demo requirements:** Your demo must only highlight the specific features, code, and functionality that you built
> during the Hackathon. Judges must be able to clearly identify what was created during the Hackathon. Failure to clearly
> identify your original contributions will result in immediate disqualification at Judges sole discretion.
>
> **New work only:** What you present at this Hackathon must have been built during the event. This should be clear in
> your submissions. **Startups may build on existing products or codebases, but you must clearly identify what was newly
> developed during the Hackathon.**
>
> **Banned projects:** Projects will be disqualified if they: violate legal, ethical, or platform policies, use code,
> data, or assets you do not have the rights to.
>
> **Sample anti-projects:** Any project using AI to generate and give medical advice e.g. AI Mental Health Advisor, skin
> blemish detector, etc.; Basic RAG Applications; Simple Image Analyzers; simple "AI for Education" Chatbots; AI Job
> Application Screener; AI Nutrition Coach; Personality Analyzers; Sports analyzers or coaches; Anything political or
> involving war

**What this means for us:**
- As a startup we may build on earlier work. We must say exactly what is new, which the README's "Built during the
  event, and what came before" section does.
- The video may show only what was built during the event.
- The building period is October 27 to November 4.

## Submission (section 7)

> Solo participants, teams and startups must submit here by November 4 at 9:00 PM EST. … Teams and startups submit one
> project … Each team must appoint one individual (the "Representative") … Please double-check that your repository is
> accessible publicly, your video link is accessible, and everyone who worked on the project is listed on the submission
> page.
>
> Your submission will require a **three minute demo video** highlighting the specific features, code, and functionality
> you built during the Hackathon. This video will also be used as one of the primary artifacts Judges use to judge your
> project, along with your code and live product.

The submission page is https://cerebralvalley.ai/e/meta-ai-developer-hackathon/hackathon/submit. Tuesday November 3 is
the last full day for submission questions.

## Judging (section 8)

1. **Pass/fail:** the project "reasonably fits the theme and reasonably used Meta Model API and/or Meta's open-weight
   models to build the project in the Hackathon".
2. **Scored 0–10:**
   - **Impact 30%:** "Who benefits … Could this actually become something people use? Does it fit into one of the problem
     statements?"
   - **Use of Meta AI Tooling 25%:** "Did they go beyond a basic integration? Did they surface capabilities that surprised
     even us?"
   - **Technical Depth & Execution 20%.**
   - **Demo 25%:** "Does the product hold up live? Is the demo video genuinely cool to watch?"
3. **The final round:** the top 5 of each track; the judges watch the videos live.

## Official FAQ answers (Discord #faqs, read 2026-10-27)

**Building on earlier work.** Kai (Cerebral Valley), October 26:
> **Using Prior Codebase / Projects:** Your submitted repo must be a standalone extract from your existing project.
> Repo: A fresh repo containing only work done during the event. Keep your existing codebase out of it.
> Demo: Open your 3-minute video with one line stating what was pre-existing and what you built this week.

> Correction: building period is officially after the kickoff session!

**Public code** (the FAQ): "Yes. Your repository must be publicly accessible."
- **When, answered in #questions** (2026-10-27, an automated reply from the Cerebral Valley app): "Yes. The repo only has
  to be public at submission — it must be publicly accessible for judges, and private repos with judge access are not
  accepted. Keep everything the app needs to run in one public repo when you submit."
- **For us:** the repo stays private until November 4, then goes public before we submit. Everything the robot needs to
  run is in this one repository. The models are downloaded from their public pages; nothing else is needed to run the
  robot.

**Our entry is clear of the anti-project list** (organizer ruling, posted by the Cerebral Valley app in our #questions
thread, 2026-10-30, 6:14 PM ET):
> "The organizer ruling covers this. A companion robot is not on the anti-project list. The medical advice restriction
> covers advice, interpretations or recommendations nobody is accountable for, so a robot that finds objects,
> remembers where things are, delivers reminders the person set and gives no medical, medication or mental-health
> advice stays clear of it. Make the physical robot and what sets it apart the core of the project, and say plainly in
> your README and demo that it gives no health advice."
- **For us:** the README says it near the top, and the video says it in one line (docs/demo-script.md).

**Only Meta's models while the project runs** (answered in #questions, 2026-10-30, 4:43 PM ET, by the Cerebral Valley
app, to "Would using multiple models and external inference providers have any negative impact on judging?"):
> "Using other AI models alongside Meta's is not allowed at runtime. Every model your project calls at runtime must be
> a Meta model, so Gemini can't be part of what the project runs, even alongside Meta open-weight models. Meta models
> can be combined with each other, and open-source Meta models count however you run them, including through partner
> inference. You can still use any coding tools during development, such as Claude Code or Copilot, without
> disclosing them. Say in your README and demo which Meta models you use and where they run."
- **For us:** every model Andy runs is Meta's except its voice, Kokoro-82M (Meta's Model API has no text-to-speech).
  The voice moves to one of Meta's own open text-to-speech models (MMS-TTS or SeamlessM4T v2). Runtime software that
  isn't a model (llama.cpp, the robot's SDK, the browser) isn't affected.

**Spend limits** (the FAQ): "Don't assume a 'Spend Limit' notification automatically stops billing." That's why our
code has its own hard cap (`heyandy_meta/budget.py`).

**Collaborators:** everyone involved in making the project must be an approved participant.
